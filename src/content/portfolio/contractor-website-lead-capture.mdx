Ferry Construction & Excavation does good work — excavation, roofing, tree work and more across
the St. Joseph and Kansas City area. But online, the business was a Facebook page. There was no
site to send people to, and no record of who reached out or how.

## What I built

A site about **his** work, not filler. Every service page is built around real job photos and
what the business actually does — no stock photos, no invented project history, no made-up team.

The part that matters most for the business is behind the scenes:

- **Every lead is captured.** Contact-form submissions land in a Google Sheet and are emailed to
  the owner right away.
- **Calls and texts are tracked too.** When someone taps the phone number or the text button, that
  gets logged — so the owner can see which pages actually produce calls.
- **Service-area pages for the towns he works in**, each written to stand on its own instead of
  the copy-paste town pages most contractor sites use.
- **An automated check on every build** catches broken links, missing image descriptions, and any
  claim the business can't back up before it goes live.

It's hosted with no monthly platform fee, so there's nothing to cancel and nothing to renew but
the domain.

## The result

The site is live at [ferryconstructionexcavation.com](https://ferryconstructionexcavation.com),
and the lead flow — form, sheet, email, call and text tracking — was tested end to end before
launch.

## Why this matters for a business like yours

Most small businesses don't have a lead problem so much as a lead-*tracking* problem: calls come
in, forms get missed, and nobody knows which marketing is working. Capturing every inquiry in one
place is usually the first system worth building — everything else (follow-ups, reminders,
reporting) builds on top of it.
